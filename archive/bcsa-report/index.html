BCSA Impact Report

A report on Based Creators Southern Africa covering chapter operations and community growth across the 2025–2026 cycle.

Redactions in this archived copy

Three categories of content from the original have been withheld:

  • Quantitative claims. The original reported membership counts, group counts, an engagement figure, and a number of grant nominations. None have been independently verified, so they are not restated. Publishing them behind a disclaimer would still be publishing them.
  • Third-party names. The original named individual contributors and described their work and support. Those names are not republished without their agreement.
  • Forward-looking sections. The original's expansion pipeline and next steps described intentions at the time of writing. They are not reproduced as achievements.

The unredacted original remains in this repository's version history. If the figures can be substantiated and the named contributors agree to be credited, a fuller version can be restored.

Summary as published

BCSA began as a grassroots regional initiative and grew into a creator and builder community operating within the wider Base ecosystem, as a regional chapter of the Based Creators network. Its stated purpose was to support local creators and builders through onboarding, education and coordination.

Regional strategy

The chapter's approach was to establish activity around universities and regional centres, where young creators and builders could learn onchain tools in a structured setting rather than alone. The report presented university-linked activity as the primary route to reaching new builders.

The report also cited a community-built onchain music application as an example of what chapter-level development could produce. Note: that application was not Bobbi Andrea's project. It is recorded as third-party work, and no claim of ownership or authorship is made here.

Ecosystem support and contributor-led outcomes

The report described BCSA as operating within a wider sub-DAO network, and described support as being directed toward active contributors and practical community outcomes — university onboarding, creator education, documentation, artist coordination and local creator-hub experiments — rather than as centralised organisational funding. The individual contributors named in the original are not named here.

Programmes

The chapter ran recurring community formats: a creator education and publishing session, a regular community discussion, cross-border coordination between regional groups, and university onboarding and builder orientation tracks.
